A session listens while your app is on screen. To keep listening after the
user leaves the app, each platform needs some setup in your app. The package
does not do this for you: it changes your app's permissions, and both app
stores ask you to justify it.

The [example app](https://github.com/ishangavidusha/transcribe_cpp/tree/main/example)
does it on both platforms.

## iOS

Add the `audio` background mode to `ios/Runner/Info.plist`:

```xml
<key>UIBackgroundModes</key>
<array>
  <string>audio</string>
</array>
```

That is all. While the session is listening, iOS keeps your app running and
shows its microphone indicator. Calls and Siri still interrupt it, and it
resumes afterwards.

Start the session while your app is on screen: iOS does not let an app start
recording from the background. App Review expects background audio to serve a
feature the user can see.

## Android

Android silences a background app's microphone after a few seconds, and the
session reports `interrupted`, unless the app runs a *foreground service* of
type `microphone`, which shows a notification while it runs.

<Steps>

1. Declare the service and its permissions in
   `android/app/src/main/AndroidManifest.xml`:

   ```xml
   <uses-permission android:name="android.permission.FOREGROUND_SERVICE"/>
   <uses-permission android:name="android.permission.FOREGROUND_SERVICE_MICROPHONE"/>
   <uses-permission android:name="android.permission.POST_NOTIFICATIONS"/>

   <application ...>
       <service
           android:name=".ListeningService"
           android:exported="false"
           android:foregroundServiceType="microphone"/>
   </application>
   ```

2. Add the service. It only shows the notification; the session keeps doing
   the listening. Copy the example's
   [`ListeningService.kt`](https://github.com/ishangavidusha/transcribe_cpp/blob/main/example/android/app/src/main/kotlin/com/transcribecpp/transcribe_cpp_example/ListeningService.kt)
   into your app's Kotlin folder, changing the package name.

3. Start it right after `await session.start()` and stop it after
   `await session.stop()`, through a method channel. The example's
   [`MainActivity.kt`](https://github.com/ishangavidusha/transcribe_cpp/blob/main/example/android/app/src/main/kotlin/com/transcribecpp/transcribe_cpp_example/MainActivity.kt)
   and
   [`background.dart`](https://github.com/ishangavidusha/transcribe_cpp/blob/main/example/lib/background.dart)
   show how.

</Steps>

<Aside type="caution">
The order matters. Since Android 14, a microphone service can only start while
your app is on screen and already has the microphone permission, which is
exactly the situation after `start()` returns. Started from the background, it
is refused.
</Aside>

From Android 13 the notification needs the `POST_NOTIFICATIONS` permission to
be visible; the service works without it, but the user will not see why the
microphone is on. Google Play asks apps with a microphone foreground service to
explain it in the Play Console.
